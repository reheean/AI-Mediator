Why Mild?
The ESRB defines "Cartoon Violence" as "violent actions involving cartoon-like situations and characters" where "a character is unharmed after the action has been inflicted." TV Parental Guidelines place this under TV-Y7: "mild fantasy violence or comedic violence" appropriate for children 7+. BBFC notes that "stylised, comic" violence is the lowest-concern tier when classifying violence.

Why High?
The ESRB defines "Violence" (unqualified) as "scenes involving aggressive conflict" and places it at the Teen (13+) rating tier. TV Parental Guidelines assign "V" at TV-14, meaning "parents are strongly urged to exercise greater care" and children under 14 should not watch unattended. BBFC considers "realistic" violence a key escalating factor in classification.

Why High?
BBFC explicitly classifies "threat and horror" as a distinct category, stating there "may be strong threat and horror" at the 15 certificate level. Their 2024 guidelines note audiences are "more concerned about depictions of violence on screen" that are "distressing or disturbing." TV Parental Guidelines flag this type of content under TV-14 requiring strong parental caution.

Why Severe?
The ESRB defines "Intense Violence" as "graphic and realistic-looking depictions of physical conflict" that "may involve extreme and/or realistic blood, gore, weapons and depictions of human injury and death" — reserved for Mature 17+ and Adults Only 18+ ratings. TV Parental Guidelines assign "graphic violence (V)" exclusively to TV-MA, the highest adult-only tier.
